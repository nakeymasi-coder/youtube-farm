How the YouTube farm works

The farm takes a run from what the Scout finds popping on YouTube right now — adapted to your channel, your audience and your voice — to a finished YouTube video, and brings the video back to you in chat for approval. One dashboard holds it all: the full Video Intake Form, the Character Vault for locked characters, solo forms for all 5 agents, and the Queue for every run and solo job.

YouTube difference: Long-form videos are built 16:9 for watch pages. YouTube Shorts are built 9:16. Character references stay 9:16 either way — headshots and body reference sheets are portrait locks. Thumbnails are 16:9 (1280 × 720).

The 5 agents, in order

1. YouTube Scout

Researches YouTube first — before anything is made. Searches your niche and the lanes next to it to see what's viral and what's popping right now: fast-rising Shorts, breakout long-form videos, titles and hooks pulling views, and the thumbnail styles on the winners. It also checks your own channel to see what has already popped for you. Then it comes back and tells you — a ranked report with the actual video titles, channels, and the view/recency evidence exactly as shown on YouTube that day, why each one is popping, and the angle adapted for your women-40+ audience in your voice — never a copy of another creator's video. You pick the direction from the report, then the farm goes from there. No invented view counts or stats — if it wasn't seen on YouTube, it isn't in the report.

2. Image & Thumbnail Agent

Built from what's popping — never generic. Works from the Scout's report: the image concepts and the 16:9 thumbnail follow the patterns on the viral videos right now — the face size and expression, the 3–4 big words, the contrast, the colour pop, split / before-and-after layouts when those are winning — translated into your brand (sapphire, chrome, pearl, butterflies) and your faces. Creates the person and avatar images too: ultra realistic, real skin texture and real hair texture, all in chat, all PNG; character references 9:16. Podcast thumbnails always show both hosts of that show — Renee + Marcus, or Simone + Desi, never mixed.

3. Script Agent

Writes in your voice — warm, plain English, no tech talk, “boo,” step-by-step with a hands-on demo where the mode calls for one. Writes the first-30-seconds hook, the full spoken script, chapter breaks and the shot list. For AI After 40 and The Prompt Breakdown it writes real host dialogue — every line is a line a host actually says, set up as its own talking shot. Labels every scene with the run's one Product Presentation choice, and writes scene action only for video prompts, never a description of the character.

4. Production Agent

Makes the video right here in Muse with the built-in video tool, in chat — the way you actually make your videos. Both character references go into every scene; show episodes are built line by line as real talking shots from the approved script, in the show's locked studio. Long-form 16:9, Shorts 9:16. For tutorials and lives that you record, it builds your filming order and screen-demo checklist instead. Muse's video tool runs on a daily allowance that refills — not credits. Returns the finished video in chat for approval. Never posts or publishes it.

5. Publish & Monetize Agent

Writes the title (built from the title patterns the Scout found popping, honest, no false claims), the full description, chapters / timestamps, tags, a pinned comment, an end-screen line pointing to your next video, and a Community post draft. The description carries your money path: the video's product / Payhip link first, the AI After 40 Study Companion ($27) on podcast episodes, then Join = the $160 Glam Vault — never the free Skool URL. For a product launch, one code with six uses, with the code and link right in the post. No price, discount or sale claim unless a live price was actually verified, and no invented views or stats.

The 8 modes — your modes

Mode is a setting, not a separate farm. It changes what the Scout researches, the script structure, the shots, the thumbnail and the title.

1. AI Photo Prompt Short — your views lane. A Gemini-style AI portrait idea as a 9:16 Short: the image, the on-screen prompt, the reveal. Themes like your winners — fashion glam, fantasy, holiday.

2. Beginner App Build — your money lane. A step-by-step build tutorial or live, like “Build Your First App with Muse” — plain English, hands-on, they build along.

3. AI Tool Walkthrough — a beginner-friendly tour of one AI tool or new feature, like “ChatGPT Just Got a Glow-Up” — show it, don't tech-talk it.

4. AI After 40 Episode — Renee + Marcus, real talking episode from the real script, tagline “AI made simple — for life and business after 40.” Study Companion linked in the description.

5. The Prompt Breakdown Episode — Simone + Desi teaching ONE prompt recipe per episode. “Prompt It Right — Say It Right to Your AI.”

6. Product Demo & Launch — a demo of your own generator, app or template: who it's for, how to use it, how to make money with it, then the Payhip link.

7. Live Teaching / Workshop — a live teaching session or workshop and its replay: the lesson plan, the demo order, and the offer it leads to.

8. Package & Post My Existing Video — for a video you already made. The Scout checks your live video and what's popping, Image & Thumbnail makes it a thumbnail that can compete, and Publish & Monetize writes the title, description, chapters and tags — then posts it once you approve the package. No new video is made in this mode.

It's not always a product — and that's built in

The Intake form asks what this video is built around: a product / offer, images, a prompt, your existing video, just a topic, or something else. The agents adapt to that choice — a prompt video leads with the prompt and its reveal, an images video leads with the images, and only a product video leads with the product and its Payhip link.

Already made the video? That's a mode, not a new agent

You asked whether checking a video you already have live needs its own agent — it doesn't. It's Mode 8, Package & Post My Existing Video, and your same 5 agents do it in a different order: Scout opens your live video and checks it against what's popping (title pulling? thumbnail competing? description doing its job?), Image & Thumbnail builds a fresh thumbnail from the winning patterns, Script is skipped (the video's already made), Production is skipped too, and Publish & Monetize writes the whole package — title, description, chapters, tags, pinned comment — and posts it after you approve the package in chat. Approving the package is your post approval for this mode; nothing posts before that.

The Product Presentation rule — either / or, never both

Every run picks one single choice, right after Mode: Wearing the product or Holding the product.

Wearing run — every prompt ends: Wearing the product, not holding it.
Holding run — every prompt ends: Holding the product, not wearing it.

Never wearing an outfit while holding that same outfit on a hanger. Reject and regenerate any image that shows both. If a video needs both, holding first and wearing later — use separate scenes, never the same shot.

The thumbnail rule

One thumbnail per long-form video, based on the thumbnail patterns the Scout found on the videos popping right now — never a generic template. The locked character's (or both hosts') real face from the references, 3–4 big words max, readable on a phone, no tiny text, no face that isn't hers, your sapphire / chrome / pearl brand with a butterfly when it fits. Made in chat as a 16:9 PNG and returned with the video for your approval — never uploaded without it.

How Run in Chat works

Every Queue run gets a Run in Chat button. Pressing it sets that run to working and opens a Run in Chat card showing the locked character, Slot 1 headshot and Slot 2 single 9:16 body reference sheet status and location with thumbnails when uploaded, the voice, the mode, the Product Presentation choice and its exact prompt sentence, the YouTube format, the topic, the length, the product name and link, and the reference video link when present. The card has a Copy prompt button. You paste that prompt into a new chat and Muse runs that exact run — nothing swapped. There is no scheduled pickup. Do not build one unless asked for separately.

The workflow, step by step

  1. Scout first — and it reports back to you. The YouTube Scout researches what's viral and popping right now in your niche, checks what has popped on your own channel, and brings you a ranked report: real titles, channels, view/recency evidence as shown on YouTube, why each is popping, and the angle adapted for your audience. You pick the direction — then the farm goes from there.
  2. Lock the look in chat. Using the locked character's reference photos, put that character in the exact look / outfit from the picked angle in chat first. Check it for face match and outfit match.
  3. Make the outfit reference sheet in chat. From that approved outfit photo, create one single 9:16 reference sheet with front, profile and three-quarter shots of her in that outfit, all on the same photo. Save it as PNG.
  4. Load both references into every scene. Reference One is her headshot. Reference Two is that chat-made outfit reference sheet. Never make a scene without both.
  5. Create the scenes in Muse with the built-in video maker, from that headshot plus sheet lock, in script order — 16:9 for long-form, 9:16 for Shorts. Show episodes: every script line is its own talking shot. Tutorials/lives you record: follow the filming order and screen-demo checklist instead.
  6. Make the thumbnail in chat — 16:9 PNG, built from the Scout's viral thumbnail patterns in your brand, her face (or both hosts), 3–4 big words.
  7. Export and return the video + thumbnail in chat for approval. Do not post, publish or share it.

The character lock in Muse — every scene, every time

  1. Every scene is made in Muse with the built-in video maker, in chat — no outside video app.
  2. Reference One: headshot. Reference Two: the chat-made outfit reference sheet. Both references go into every scene generation — never a scene without both.
  3. No character bible is needed. The reference photos are the lock. Do not paste a bible into the scene prompts.
  4. Show episodes stay in their locked studio, with their own hosts only — Renee + Marcus for AI After 40, Simone + Desi for The Prompt Breakdown, never mixed.

Use this prompt shape for every scene — and nothing else about her

Use the reference images and keep the character perfectly consistent. [Scene action only.] [Spoken line, if any.] Wearing the product, not holding it.

Never describe her in the prompt. No face, hair, skin, body or outfit description. Describing her overrides the references and creates a different girl. The last line swaps to the holding version when that is the run's choice.

Standing rules

Image format: Every image Muse creates must be PNG, not webp — character images, outfit references, outfit reference sheets and thumbnails. Your own uploaded Vault photos stay as you saved them, JPG or PNG. Webp uploads are rejected with a clear message.

Characters: Ultra realistic, with real skin texture and real hair texture. Character A is reused as Character A, Character B as Character B. Within one video, identity, clothing, location and lighting stay frozen from the uploaded reference photos.

Scout-first rule: No run starts production on a guess. The Scout researches YouTube live, reports what's popping with the evidence as shown, and Glam picks the direction before Image, Script and Production build anything.

Permissions: Videos are made in Muse in chat — there is no outside video app to upload to in this farm. Posting or publishing to YouTube still needs your separate approval, every time.

Video allowance: Muse's video tool runs on a daily allowance that refills on its own — not credits. There is nothing to buy.

Queue rule: The Queue status button only changes the label to queued, working or done. It does not run YouTube research, image generation or video making. Real execution is triggered from chat, using Run in Chat.
